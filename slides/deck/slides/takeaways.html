TAKEAWAYS

Learning helps where the channel set is nonlinear and pilots are scarce

1

Within a narrow block, use LMMSE with trained statistics: no MLP was better in 84 settings, and LMMSE gained 1.7–9.7 dB over linear interpolation.

2

If the noise level is unreliable, a blind MLP costs about 0.3 dB or less up to 20 dB.

3

Across antennas, a GMM estimator gains up to 5.9 dB over joint LMMSE, at up to 64× the computation.

Limits: one room, synthetic pilot noise, one test trajectory.

To summarise. One: inside a narrow frequency block the measured channel is almost linear, so LMMSE with good statistics is the right tool and the neural network adds nothing. Two: the network's real benefit is robustness when the noise level is unknown. Three: learning does help in the joint antenna setting, where the set of channels is nonlinear and the pilots leave the channel underdetermined, but at a clear computational cost. Limitations: one room, synthetic noise, and one test trajectory in the same room. Thank you; we are happy to take questions. (About 50 seconds.)
