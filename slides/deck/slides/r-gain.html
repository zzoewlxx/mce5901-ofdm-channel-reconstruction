Across 4 variants, 3 pilot densities and 7 SNRs, that is 84 settings, and in none of them is an MLP better under our frozen rule. Up to 15 dB they are within 0.2 dB. At high SNR the plain MLP loses about 1 to 1.6 dB: its own approximation error becomes visible. The residual MLP learns a correction of essentially zero. The reason is in the data: inside a 3 MHz block, two eigenmodes of the covariance carry 99.99 percent of the energy. The channel there is almost a two-dimensional linear family, and for that LMMSE with half a million training vectors is already nearly optimal. (About 75 seconds.)
