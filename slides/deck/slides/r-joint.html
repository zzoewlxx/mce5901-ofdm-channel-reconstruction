RESULT 4 · TEST RUN, JOINT SETTING

Across antennas, a learned prior helps

The GMM estimator is better than joint LMMSE in 8 of 8 settings: +2.9 to +5.9 dB with pilots on 8 antennas. The price: up to 64× the multiply–accumulates, 0.76 ms instead of 12 µs per estimate.

Group 12 · OFDM channel reconstruction

9

Now the joint setting over 32 antennas. Here the picture changes. With pilots on only 8 of the 32 antennas, joint LMMSE cannot extrapolate to the other antennas, and the Gaussian-mixture estimator gains 3 to almost 6 dB; the joint MLPs gain 2 to 5 dB. With pilots on all antennas the GMM still gains 2 dB at low SNR. The plain joint MLP has an error floor at high SNR; the residual version fixes that. The GMM is better than joint LMMSE in all 8 settings. Two cautions: with pilots on 8 antennas the absolute error is still high, minus 5 to minus 9 dB, and the GMM costs up to 64 times the computation. (About 75 seconds.)
